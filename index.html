FIELD BRIEF · 01UPPER-INTERMEDIATE

Managing water
across a watershed

Water does not follow administrative boundaries. It moves through landscapes, connecting upstream forests, farms, cities and coastal ecosystems.

Integrated water resources management (IWRM) is a process that promotes the coordinated development and management of water, land and related resources. Its purpose is to maximize social and economic well-being without compromising the sustainability of vital ecosystems.

In practice, this means bringing different voices to the same table. Local communities, government agencies, farmers and technical teams may hold different priorities, yet all depend on the same water system. A technical assistant can help translate field evidence into clear information, support dialogue and monitor agreed actions.

Good decisions also rely on good data. Rainfall records, water-quality samples and community observations can reveal changes over time. When information is shared in an accessible way, stakeholders are better equipped to identify risks and plan together.
